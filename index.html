How the labels are made
a / t
Median charge divided by the revealed fair threshold t (middle of its interval), on lines that are covered (t > 0).
Above t
Share of their charges on covered lines that were proven above t (paid nothing if rejected, capped if accepted).
Overcharger
a/t ≥ 1 and at least half of the charges above t.
Threshold hunter
Prices close to t: a/t ≥ 0.75 and more than 30% above t.
Fair pricer
a/t ≥ 0.75 and at most 30% above t.
Undercutter
a/t < 0.75: almost always fair, gives away margin.
Paid as insurer
Everything the team paid out as insurer in the analysed games.
Strict / Lenient
Accept rate at least 5 points below / above the field median. Otherwise Selective.
Fair accepted
Share of that total paid for fair bills they accepted (a ≤ t): money that is owed anyway.
Inflated paid
Share paid for bills they accepted although the charge was above t (the cost of being too lenient).
Fair rejected
Share paid as penalties for fair bills they rejected (1.5a each: the cost of being too strict).
Unclassified
Accepted bills on lines whose t is not pinned down yet; shown grey in the bar.
Window
All figures and labels use only the last 10 completed games, so they describe how teams play now, not earlier in the tournament.
Last N games
The same labels on the most recent games only. Shown when the strategy changed.

3 · From estimate to (a, b)

Per invoice line. The insurer limit is opponent-independent; the charge is a best response to how the others accept.

Green: insurer role (acceptance limit b). Blue: handyman role (charge a). The ⅓ quantile follows from the payoff table: accepting a fair bill costs a, wrongly rejecting it costs 1.5a, so accepting pays off once P(t ≥ a) ≥ ⅔.
